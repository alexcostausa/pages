Research: Impact of Bolsonaro's Presidency on LGBTQ+ Rights and Safety in Brazil

Research Scope

This document examines the impact of former President Jair Bolsonaro's rhetoric, policies, and actions on LGBTQ+ individuals in Brazil during his presidency (2019-2022), including pre-election statements, their resonance with voters, measurable impacts during his term, and potential long-term consequences.

1. Pre-Election Rhetoric and Voter Resonance

1.1 Historical Statements

Prior to his election in 2018, Jair Bolsonaro made numerous public statements regarding LGBTQ+ individuals:

  • In a 2011 interview with Playboy magazine, Bolsonaro stated he would be "incapable of loving a homosexual son" and that he would prefer his son to die in an accident rather than be gay
  • In 2013, he stated on television that the government should not provide financial support for campaigns against homophobia
  • Multiple statements characterizing LGBTQ+ individuals as threats to "traditional family values"
  • Opposition to same-sex marriage and adoption rights for same-sex couples
  • Claims that LGBTQ+ education materials in schools were part of a "gay agenda" to indoctrinate children

1.2 Electoral Appeal

Key Finding: Bolsonaro's conservative social platform, including anti-LGBTQ+ rhetoric, resonated with evangelical Christian voters, who represented approximately 30% of Brazil's population. His election with 55.1% of the vote in the 2018 runoff suggested significant support for his conservative social agenda.

Analysis of voting patterns indicated:

  • Strong support from evangelical communities who shared concerns about "traditional values"
  • Rural and conservative urban areas showed higher support rates
  • Campaign materials emphasized "God, Country, and Family" themes
  • Strategic alignment with conservative religious leaders who amplified his message

2. Impact During Presidency (2019-2022)

2.1 Violence and Safety Statistics

Data from Brazilian Organizations:

  • Grupo Gay da Bahia (GGB) - Brazil's oldest LGBTQ+ rights organization - reported significant increases in violence:
    • 2018 (pre-presidency): 420 LGBTQ+ deaths reported
    • 2019: 329 documented deaths (methodology changes noted)
    • 2020: Pandemic period saw changes in reporting patterns
    • 2021-2022: Continued elevated rates of violence and hate crimes
  • ANTRA (National Association of Travestis and Transsexuals) reported Brazil remained the country with the highest number of transgender murders globally during this period
  • Under-reporting remained a significant issue, with many incidents not captured in official statistics

2.2 Policy Actions and Government Positions

During his presidency, Bolsonaro's administration took several actions affecting LGBTQ+ rights:

  • Ministry of Human Rights: Appointed Damares Alves, a conservative evangelical pastor, who stated "boys wear blue and girls wear pink" and opposed LGBTQ+ inclusive policies
  • LGBTQ+ Department Elimination: Disbanded the LGBTQ+ department within the Ministry of Human Rights shortly after taking office
  • Budget Cuts: Reduced funding for programs supporting LGBTQ+ individuals and combating homophobia
  • Health Policies: Restricted discussions of LGBTQ+ issues in health education programs
  • Supreme Court Conflicts: Despite Supreme Court rulings protecting LGBTQ+ rights, the administration's rhetoric often contradicted these protections
  • COVID-19 Response: LGBTQ+ organizations reported that vulnerable LGBTQ+ individuals faced additional barriers accessing pandemic relief

2.3 Rhetorical Climate

Climate of Hostility: Throughout his presidency, Bolsonaro continued making statements that LGBTQ+ advocates argued normalized discrimination and violence:

  • Continued references to "gender ideology" as a threat
  • Public disagreements with Supreme Court decisions protecting LGBTQ+ rights
  • Statements suggesting LGBTQ+ acceptance led to social decay
  • Campaign materials in 2022 re-election bid continued anti-LGBTQ+ themes

2.4 Social Impacts

Research and reports from Brazilian civil society organizations documented:

  • Increased reports of bullying and discrimination in schools
  • LGBTQ+ individuals reporting feeling less safe in public spaces
  • Families feeling "empowered" to reject LGBTQ+ family members, citing presidential rhetoric as validation
  • Workplace discrimination complaints increased
  • Mental health impacts on LGBTQ+ communities, with higher rates of anxiety and depression reported

3. Long-Term Impact and Legacy

3.1 Academic and Professional Analysis

Researchers and professionals studying Brazilian politics and social movements have identified several long-term concerns:

Normalization Effect: Studies suggest that when political leaders use discriminatory rhetoric, it can normalize such attitudes in broader society, with effects lasting years after the leader leaves office.

  • Generational Impact: Children and young people exposed to anti-LGBTQ+ messaging during formative years (2019-2022) may carry these attitudes into adulthood
  • Family Dynamics: Families where members already held anti-LGBTQ+ views reported feeling "validated" and "empowered" by presidential rhetoric, potentially affecting intergenerational attitudes
  • Institutional Changes: Policy rollbacks and institutional changes may take years to reverse, even with new leadership
  • Civil Society: LGBTQ+ organizations noted that rebuilding trust and safety will require sustained effort beyond Bolsonaro's term

3.2 Timeline Estimates from Researchers

While exact predictions vary, social scientists studying similar cases globally suggest:

  • Immediate Effects (2023-2025): Continuation of normalized hostility until new norms are established through consistent counter-messaging
  • Medium-Term (5-10 years): Gradual shift in attitudes, particularly if new leadership actively promotes inclusive policies and protections
  • Long-Term (10-20 years): Some attitudes and family dynamics affected during 2019-2022 may persist, particularly in conservative communities
  • Generational: Full reversal of normalization effects may require a full generation (20-30 years) without similar rhetoric from political leadership

3.3 Comparison with Global Cases

International research on political rhetoric and minority rights suggests:

  • Countries where leaders used similar rhetoric saw effects lasting 10-15 years on average
  • Active counter-programming and education can reduce impact duration
  • Legal protections help but don't fully mitigate social acceptance changes
  • Community resilience and civil society strength are key protective factors

4. Current Political Situation and Future Scenarios

4.1 Bolsonaro's Legal Status

As of 2024, Bolsonaro faces multiple legal challenges:

  • Electoral Ineligibility: In June 2023, Brazil's Superior Electoral Court (TSE) ruled Bolsonaro ineligible to run for office until 2030 due to abuse of power and misuse of government media
  • Criminal Investigations: Multiple ongoing investigations including:
    • Alleged coup attempt and attempted subversion of democratic processes
    • Mishandling of COVID-19 pandemic
    • Alleged embezzlement of state gifts (jewelry)
    • Falsification of vaccination records
  • Potential criminal charges could result in imprisonment, though no convictions as of current data

4.2 Political Comeback Scenarios

Possible Scenarios for Political Return:

Scenario 1: Electoral Ineligibility Reversal

  • Would require Supreme Electoral Court or Supreme Federal Court to overturn the 2023 ruling
  • Considered unlikely based on Brazilian legal precedents
  • Could potentially occur if legal grounds for appeal are found

Scenario 2: Future Allies in Power

  • If Bolsonaro-aligned candidates win 2026 or 2030 elections, possibilities include:
    • Presidential pardon (if convicted of crimes)
    • Legislative changes to amnesty or annul specific charges
    • Constitutional amendments affecting eligibility rules (requires 3/5 congressional majority)
  • His sons and political allies remain active in Brazilian politics:
    • Senator Flávio Bolsonaro
    • Congressman Eduardo Bolsonaro
    • Former Rio de Janeiro councilman Carlos Bolsonaro
    • Various aligned governors and federal legislators

Scenario 3: Waiting Until 2030

  • If no convictions result in longer ineligibility, he could potentially run in 2030 (age 75)
  • Would require maintaining political relevance during 8-year hiatus
  • Historical precedent: Former President Lula returned to office after legal challenges

4.3 Impact of Potential Return

If Bolsonaro or closely aligned candidates return to power, implications for LGBTQ+ rights could include:

  • Renewed anti-LGBTQ+ rhetoric from highest levels of government
  • Reversal of any protections implemented by intervening administrations
  • Emboldening of discriminatory attitudes that may have begun to recede
  • Potential regression in violence prevention efforts
  • International implications for Brazil's human rights standing

4.4 Current Bolsonarismo Movement

Even without Bolsonaro in office, the political movement bearing his name ("Bolsonarismo") remains active:

  • Significant congressional representation (approximately 30% of federal deputies aligned)
  • Several governorships and state legislatures
  • Large social media following maintains influence
  • Conservative social agenda, including anti-LGBTQ+ positions, remains core to movement identity
  • Potential candidates for 2026 elections could carry forward similar rhetoric

5. Intergenerational Impact and Family Dynamics

5.1 Family-Level Empowerment

Social workers and LGBTQ+ support organizations reported notable changes in family dynamics:

  • Increased reports of family rejection of LGBTQ+ members, with specific references to Bolsonaro's statements as justification
  • Parents feeling "validated" in refusing to accept LGBTQ+ children
  • Extended family members feeling empowered to pressure LGBTQ+ relatives to change or hide their identities
  • Religious families citing both religious doctrine and presidential rhetoric as dual justifications

5.2 Youth and Educational Impacts

Reports from educational institutions and youth organizations indicated:

  • Increased bullying of LGBTQ+ students in schools
  • Students using presidential quotes to justify harassment
  • Teachers reporting challenges in addressing LGBTQ+ topics due to perceived political pressure
  • LGBTQ+ youth reporting higher levels of family conflict
  • Increased demand for mental health services among LGBTQ+ adolescents

5.3 Long-Term Generational Effects

Researchers studying intergenerational transmission of attitudes suggest:

  • Children (ages 0-12 during 2019-2022): May internalize attitudes from this period, affecting their adult views (15-20 year impact)
  • Adolescents (ages 13-18 during 2019-2022): Critical identity formation period; exposure to normalized discrimination may have lasting effects (10-15 year impact)
  • Young Adults (ages 19-30 during 2019-2022): More established identities but still susceptible to normalization of rhetoric (5-10 year impact)
  • Families with Shared Values: Where family members already held anti-LGBTQ+ views, presidential validation may have strengthened and entrenched these attitudes for decades

5.4 Mechanisms of Lasting Impact

Psychological and sociological research identifies several mechanisms by which political rhetoric creates lasting effects:

  1. Normalization: Repeated exposure to discriminatory language from authority figures normalizes such views
  2. Permission Structure: Political leaders' statements create a "permission structure" for expressing previously suppressed biases
  3. Identity Formation: Political affiliation becomes tied to social attitudes, making attitude change feel like identity betrayal
  4. Information Bubbles: Social media and selective news consumption can perpetuate attitudes even after political changes
  5. Community Reinforcement: Like-minded communities continue reinforcing attitudes through social interaction

6. Research Needs and Limitations

6.1 Data Challenges

Important Limitations:

  • Official government statistics on LGBTQ+ violence and discrimination are limited in Brazil
  • Under-reporting is significant due to fear, stigma, and lack of trust in authorities
  • Changes in data collection methodologies during 2019-2022 complicate longitudinal analysis
  • COVID-19 pandemic (2020-2022) affected both actual violence patterns and reporting mechanisms
  • Causal attribution is complex: while rhetoric clearly matters, isolating its effect from other factors is methodologically challenging

6.2 Ongoing Research Areas

Scholars continue investigating:

  • Long-term mental health impacts on LGBTQ+ individuals who lived through this period
  • Effectiveness of resilience and resistance strategies by LGBTQ+ communities
  • International comparisons with other countries experiencing similar political dynamics
  • Role of social media in amplifying or moderating political rhetoric effects
  • Economic impacts on LGBTQ+ individuals (employment discrimination, housing, etc.)
  • Intersection of anti-LGBTQ+ rhetoric with other forms of discrimination (racial, gender, class)

Organizations and institutions producing relevant research include:

  • ANTRA: National Association of Travestis and Transsexuals, tracks violence against trans individuals
  • ILGA: International Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Trans and Intersex Association, publishes global reports including Brazil
  • Human Rights Watch: Documents human rights situations including LGBTQ+ rights in Brazil

  • Brazilian universities: Multiple research centers studying LGBTQ+ rights, political rhetoric, and social attitudes

7. Conclusions and Summary

Key Findings Summary:

  1. Pre-Election: Bolsonaro's anti-LGBTQ+ rhetoric was well-established and resonated with conservative voters, contributing to his electoral success.
  2. During Presidency: His administration dismantled LGBTQ+ protections, cut funding, and maintained hostile rhetoric. Violence and discrimination against LGBTQ+ individuals remained at concerning levels, with Brazil continuing to lead globally in transgender murders.
  3. Social Impact: The rhetorical climate empowered individuals and families to express and act on discriminatory attitudes, with effects reported across educational, family, workplace, and public spaces.
  4. Long-Term Effects: Researchers estimate the normalization effects could last 10-30 years, with the most significant impacts on the generation that came of age during his presidency.
  5. Political Future: While Bolsonaro is currently ineligible to run until 2030, scenarios exist for earlier return through legal reversal, political amnesty, or continuation through aligned candidates. His political movement remains strong.
  6. Generational Legacy: Families that felt validated by presidential rhetoric may pass these attitudes to future generations, creating lasting impacts beyond Bolsonaro's direct political involvement.

Critical Considerations:

  • The interplay between political rhetoric and social attitudes is complex and multidirectional
  • LGBTQ+ communities demonstrated significant resilience and organized resistance throughout this period
  • Judicial protections and civil society efforts provide important counterweights to political hostility
  • Brazil's LGBTQ+ movement is long-established and continues advocating for rights and protections
  • International attention and pressure provide some protection and support
  • The trajectory is not deterministic; active efforts can mitigate long-term impacts
